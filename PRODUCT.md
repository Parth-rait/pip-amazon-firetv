## Platform

adaptive *(inferred)*: a Vega OS app on Fire TV (React Native for Vega) and a web app (the Pip Pad) on phones and tablets. In this repo the TV is also simulated in a browser for testing.

## Users

- A household watching a film together on Fire TV: children from about 7, parents, grandparents. Different ages share one screen and one decision.
- The host: whoever joined first. Holds the playback controls on their Pip Pad (a tablet by default).
- Everyone else: joins on their own phone by scanning the TV's QR code, with a first name only.

The job: watch without losing the thread, ask about what is on screen without stopping the film or leaving the room, and keep control of playback without fighting over a remote.

## Product Purpose

Pip is a context-and-action system for everyday environments. It understands enough of the current moment to help without making people re-explain it, answers from what has already been established, acts only through permitted devices and verifies that the action happened. Conversation is one interface, not the product. Fire TV with the Pip Pad is the first environment that proves this.

Success for the first release: a family joins in seconds, starts the film, asks Pip about the scene, gets a short grounded answer or an honest "not sure", dismisses it, and keeps watching, without ever being shown something that happens later.

## Positioning

Pip answers only from timestamped, reviewed evidence about what the room has already seen, enforces that boundary in code (not just in a prompt), and treats "command sent" and "command done" as different things. Neighbouring products answer from the open web or general model knowledge, or treat a remote press as the end of the story.

## Operating Context

- A living room: a TV across the room, a tablet on the sofa, phones in hands, uneven light, conversation going on.
- The film is primary. Pip stays hidden on the TV until someone asks, shows a reply for a few seconds, and leaves.
- The Fire TV remote still works for play, pause and seeking; Pip never blocks it.
- First film: Big Buck Bunny (CC BY 3.0). Test film: The Lantern Fox (our own, generated by `npm run lab:film`).

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Join by QR code or 4-character code; up to 8 players; first names only; no accounts.
- Host-only playback controls with command ids, de-duplication and confirmation from the TV's own report.
- Ask Pip by starter chips, typing or the browser's speech input; replies are answers, "Did you mean" options, or honest abstentions.
- Answers come from a reviewed timeline of facts, filtered to what has been revealed by now; future questions get a fixed no-spoiler line.
- One semantic input path for pad buttons, a touchwheel, and remote keys.
- Constraints: Node 22; one server process; Bedrock Nova 2 Lite for model answers (`us.amazon.nova-2-lite-v1:0`); the Vega app must be generated and tested on a Mac with the Vega SDK.
- Undecided: whether the opt-in family quiz ships in the hackathon build; Pip's spoken voice; any persistent memory (needs consent, provenance and deletion first).

## Brand Commitments

- One recognisable Pip: a soft mint circle with two eyes, no mouth, no limbs. Motion only shows state.
- Voice: warm, short, specific; names and numbers over adjectives; never blames; says "I'm not sure" rather than guessing. At most 35 words per answer.
- The dark matte surfaces with the single mint accent are the existing identity (PROJECT.md 3.2).

## Evidence on Hand

- Working code with 198 server tests (including 19 lab scenarios), 13 pad tests and a 14-check browser run (docs/architecture/current-state.md).
- Benchmark on the lab film: 0 spoiler leaks in 68 probe runs, including a fake model that always tells the ending (docs/evaluation/results/).
- Screenshots: docs/screenshots/.
- Absent and not to be fabricated: a reviewed Big Buck Bunny timeline, any real-model (Nova) measurement, a usability test with real people, and a real Fire TV or VVD run of the new flows.

## Product Principles

1. The film comes first: Pip appears only when asked and gets out of the way.
2. Grounded or silent: an answer cites what the room has seen, or Pip says it is not sure.
3. Done means observed: an action counts only when the device's own report shows it.
4. Everyone is equal in the room: a child and a grandparent get the same screens and the same say.
5. Private by default: names live in memory for the session; nothing about people is inferred or kept.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Large type and 56 px touch targets on the pad; TV text inside a 5% safe margin at the TV type scale; visible focus for remote and keyboard; `prefers-reduced-motion` respected; no meaning carried by colour alone; the remote always works as a fallback.
